REFERENCE / GAME I

Field manual

Play a turn
  1. Deploy 14 Red and 13 Blue divisions on their own map halves. Remaining units become reserves.
  2. Initiative is randomized every full turn. Starting on turn 4, one reserve per side per turn becomes available in that side’s capital. An enemy in any capital hex blocks arrival.
  3. Move any divisions up to their movement allowance. One ground unit fits in a hex. A unit must stop when entering an enemy zone of control (ZOC) and must disengage before moving between enemy zones.
  4. Every engaged division must take part in one battle. All attackers in a battle must border all its defenders, and the defending hexes must border each other. Assign battles or use the suggested plan.
  5. Choose tactics in secret. Read the combat result, pay losses, resolve any retreats, and then advance. The second side follows the same sequence.
Terrain, drops, and invasions

Armored and combined-arms units cannot enter woods. Only SOF can enter mountains. Sea is reserved for SOF in transit during an invasion. Outer half-hexes are out of play. Full hexes on the join are playable after deployment.

Opt into terrain effects at setup to use movement costs and defense bonuses. Woods and mountains cost 2 MF. Entering a river costs 3 MF, or 1 along the same river. A connected road costs ⅓ MF. Cities, rivers, woods and mountains double defense. Retreats and advances use 1 MF per hex, with terrain restrictions still in force.

Airborne divisions starting in a friendly city can drop into a legal vacant hex outside enemy ZOC. SOF starting in a coastal city can invade a beach. They retain their movement allowance after a free landing. An occupied beach must be attacked and secured by advance.

Combat and victory

Combat odds round down to the defender’s advantage and stay between 1:3 and 3:1. AE and DE eliminate every participating unit on that side. A4, A6 and D4 require the indicated minimum CF. Game I pays with whole divisions. The optional §23.1 procedure also lets you flip a full-strength division to half CF rounded down and one less MF.

The attacker can order all surviving defenders to retreat or leave them in place. The attacker chooses each retreat route. A required retreat with no legal route eliminates the unit.

The optional mandatory-retreat procedure makes the defense card's retreat compulsory and allows the attacker to withdraw their own survivors. Advances follow retreats and withdrawals.

If an inherited engagement deadlock survives movement, the app can resolve the largest legal set of contacts. The battle planner and field report list any divisions exempted by this table ruling.

Victory is checked after both sides act. If either side has less than 20 CF on the board, the stronger side wins. A side also wins by capturing and occupying every enemy city. Capture requires holding the hex through an opposing combat phase.

Solitaire, computer, hot-seat, and LAN

In solitaire you command both sides. Manual deployment randomizes the next side. The supplied solitaire table rolls the defensive tactic each battle. You can choose or roll the offensive tactic.

Vs computer gives you one faction. The local opponent chooses moves and tactics using the same legal actions as a human and has no access to your concealed deployment or tactic. Both sides choose tactics as in hot-seat play.

Hot-seat hands the screen to the other player for private deployment and tactic choices. Each player uses their own browser in LAN play. Create a room on the host and share its invite with your opponent on the same network. Keep the host running.

Sources and scope

Game I and the core Game III solitaire procedure follow Christian Holm Christensen’s 2023 re-typeset edition of Avalon Hill’s Kriegspiel. The original game was designed by Thomas N. Shaw. Map and counter art from the re-typeset edition is credited to Christian Holm Christensen under CC BY-SA 4.0.

Terrain effects, fixed or full deployment, and step loss by factors are selectable Game II options. Additional Game II modules are grouped by section in game setup. Game IV uses a separate scenario.
